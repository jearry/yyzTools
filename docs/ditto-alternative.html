A Ditto alternative — clipboard history that reads your images

Ditto is a beloved open-source clipboard manager that keeps and searches whatever you copied. yyzTools keeps and searches your clipboard too, then does something Ditto can't: it OCRs copied images, so their text becomes searchable and copyable. It sits inside a free Alt+Space toolbox with a launcher, full-disk file search, OCR, translation and cleanup. Ditto wins on open source and LAN sync; yyzTools wins when you want your clipboard plus the rest of the toolkit in one app.

Every copied image becomes searchable text

A screenshot, a meme, a photo of a whiteboard — once you copy it, yyzTools runs OCR in the background and stores the recognized text with the image. Days later you can search the clipboard by a word that only appears inside that picture, and copy just the text. Ditto saves images perfectly well; it just never reads them.

When Ditto is the better pick

If open source, LAN / multi-machine sync, a command-line interface, or power-user paste controls are what you actually use, Ditto is the more capable and more transparent tool — free, GPL-licensed and battle-tested by years of daily use. yyzTools is a general toolbox with clipboard history in it, not a specialist clipboard app.

When yyzTools fits better

If you'd rather have your clipboard history inside the same Alt+Space box that already runs your launcher, full-disk file search, OCR and cleanup — and you want copied images to become searchable text — one yyzTools install covers it, and it's free for work use too.

Common questions

Is yyzTools a good replacement for Ditto?

For everyday clipboard history and search, yes. Ditto stays ahead on open source, LAN sync and CLI/scriptable paste; yyzTools goes past Ditto on one thing — OCR on copied images — and folds the clipboard into a full toolbox. Pick by which side of that line matters to you.

Can it really search text inside images I copied?

Yes. When you copy an image that contains text (a screenshot or an image file), yyzTools runs OCR and stores the text with it. Later you can search the clipboard by a word that only appears inside that image, and copy just the recognized text. Ditto stores the image but does not read it.

Should I uninstall Ditto before installing yyzTools?

You can run both, but two clipboard managers may grab the same hotkey and duplicate history. It's usually cleaner to keep one — Ditto for a dedicated open-source clipboard tool, yyzTools if you want the clipboard folded into the whole toolbox.
