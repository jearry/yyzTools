ShareX is the power user's screenshot Swiss army knife — deeply configurable, open source, with an upload workflow second to none. yyzTools' capture module covers the same daily ground — region, scrolling capture, annotation, pin to screen — and goes where ShareX doesn't: a local OCR engine bundled in the installer, and translation one click from the capture toolbar.

OCR that ships in the box — and translation next to it

ShareX's OCR rides on Windows' built-in engine, so a new language means installing a system language pack first. yyzTools bundles the RapidOCR engine and its models in the installer: it works the moment you install, handles mixed scripts, and the capture toolbar's OCR and translate buttons (or the standalone Quick OCR, Alt+T) turn a snip into translated text without leaving the flow.

When ShareX is still the right pick

If your screenshots are headed somewhere — a wide range of upload destinations, custom uploaders, after-capture task chains, auto capture — or you want every knob exposed in an open-source, free, no-account tool, ShareX remains the power user's choice, with one of the largest communities in Windows freeware.

When yyzTools is the better fit

If your screenshots are for reading and replying — text to extract, a passage to translate, a long page to capture — yyzTools finishes the job inside the capture window, and the same install already carries the launcher, file search, clipboard history and batch tools, 387 modules in total.

Common questions

Does yyzTools replace ShareX?

On everyday capture — region, scrolling, annotation, pinning — largely yes, and it adds translation and a bundled OCR engine. On ShareX's home turf, no: upload destinations, custom uploaders and after-capture automation have no equivalent here. Heavy sharers should keep ShareX.

How do the OCR engines differ?

yyzTools ships the RapidOCR engine and models inside the installer, so recognition works out of the box and is tuned for mixed scripts. ShareX calls Windows' built-in OCR, whose languages follow your installed system language packs — adding one means a trip to Windows language settings.

Can both stay installed together?

Yes. If any hotkeys collide, remap them on either side (yyzTools: tray icon → Settings → Shortcuts).
